01Simulation

Timing & world height

Fixed tick comes from the install. Fire advances once immediately using its callback's deltaTime; first tick defaults to the fixed tick, but can differ in-game. Solution cost scales roughly with 1/tick, and the range card is solved once per row, so a very fine tick with a small table interval is the expensive combination. The solve runs in a background worker where the browser supports one.

03Muzzle devices

    04Sight & range

    Defaults to the assumed midpoint (50%). 0% is the rear endpoint; 100% is the front. Adjust to match your actual mounting position.

    Forward-facing direct mounting, with no optic cant relative to the weapon. Whole-weapon cant is set below. Matching mount types does not check collisions or occupied slots. Adapters, risers and reversed mounting require manual geometry.

    Values describe the optical origin relative to the bare weapon muzzle and remain editable. Selected devices add their forward shift to setback and subtract their up shift from sight height automatically. No device: these are the effective values.

    Sight-line angle above horizontal: 0° is level, positive is uphill, negative is downhill. The barrel also includes the optic-zero adjustment; elevation corrections are relative to this sight line.

    Viewed through the optic: positive tilts weapon-up right, negative tilts it left. This fixed cant is included when solving elevation and windage.

    Nominal cant is 0°. Samples tilts across this interval without changing the nominal dial settings. Shows a geometric uncertainty envelope, not a statistical confidence interval.

    Specific cant and cant uncertainty are mutually exclusive. These are assumed inputs; the calculator cannot detect live weapon cant.

    The solve always works in distance measured along the sight line from the optical origin. Choosing a horizontal distance converts it by dividing by the cosine of the firing angle.

    Range-card rows are solved independently, one aim solve per row.

    The projectile path is simulated after applying the solved aim corrections. The scope adjustments above use the opposite signs. The straight dashed ray is the original, uncorrected POA at the base optic setting, rotated with the weapon into the corrected view. It is not the zero-offset corrected aim axis. Its base-zero marker is a reference, not the corrected shot's target. An on-wall POA marker and separate height/lateral guides show its distance from corrected impact; off-wall intersections get neither marker nor guides. RISE or DROP, DRIFT and ERROR are shown beside the back wall in the same colors as their measurement lines. RISE means impact above uncorrected POA; DROP means below. These and DRIFT are geometric offsets, not dial settings. ERROR is lateral cant uncertainty in cm; ± gives the error on each side, not total width. CONE is the full-disc bound of the game's launch dispersion at this range, not a measured group; it is drawn on the wall only when it fits, and the readout keeps the figure when it does not. When enabled, the purple band shows sampled accidental-cant uncertainty with the displayed dials held fixed. Only the ray extends if the zero is beyond the selected range. The height ceiling is the plotted projectile apex; the POA ray and base-zero marker are clipped to this height range rather than stretching it. An arrowhead at the clipped ray end indicates that the POA continues off the plot. Off-wall distances remain in the side readout. Height/lateral scales are exaggerated; the grid is the corrected sight plane, not terrain.

    Model, accuracy & local data

    This calculator uses numeric parameters extracted from this installation and a port of its free-flight projectile update. It is for H3VR, not real firearms. It does not simulate impacts, penetration, ricochets, dispersion, guided projectiles, or modded behavior.

    Optic zero setting is an input, not an imposed trajectory crossing. Modern vanilla PIP scopes and reflex sights use the caliber's authored BulletDropCurve at setting × 0.001, independent of the loaded round's speed, mass or ballistic gravity mode. Legacy holo/red-dot sights instead point at a geometric bore-line target. Calculated zero is a separate mode that solves a crossing for the selected round.

    Weapon presets use the stock prefab's chamber and muzzle transform positions (including parent rotation and scale), serialized chamber velocity multiplier, and verified shot-call multiplier. Select a chamber/barrel for multi-chamber weapons. Geometry is from the authored prefab pose, not live animation; attachments, moving parts or mods may require overrides. Missing geometry or unverified dynamic shot paths are flagged for manual input. Sight geometry remains a separate input.

    The muzzle-device loadout includes suppressors, brakes, extenders, shrouds and other catalogued devices. Fixed drift comes from the last registered device and the weapon/device identities; drop multipliers combine. Stock mount geometry uses the active device muzzle, mount rotation, root mount scale and verified submount chain. Moving, reversible, off-axis or unverified mounts require measured geometry for a centered, forward/bore-aligned muzzle. Selecting a catalogued device does not prove it fits the weapon. Random device spread is not simulated.

    Sight geometry assumes a centered, bore-aligned optic with no initial elevation/windage dial trim. Enter optical-origin height and setback relative to the bare weapon muzzle, not the outer lens position. Selected device geometry supplies the change to the active muzzle; the model and CSV use the resulting effective values. Nominal optic distance is measured along the bore from the muzzle; chart/table range is distance along the selected sight line from the optical origin. Firing angle tilts that sight line relative to world horizontal; gravity remains world-down. Chart/table height is perpendicular to the sight line, and elevation corrections are relative to it. Optic-zero angles remain relative to the sight line, not world horizontal. Scene catch-height and world-height limits still apply to inclined shots. Random range extension is set to its minimum; tables show primary centerline flight only, not spread or submunition paths.

    Weapon cant rolls the centered weapon/optic setup about the sight ray; positive cant tilts weapon-up right as viewed through the optic. Specific cant is included in the two-axis solve. Alternatively, cant uncertainty samples 21 angles across a nonzero ± tolerance around a level nominal setup, keeping its solved dials fixed. The purple envelope shows sampled geometric variation, not random spread or a statistical confidence interval. If any sampled tilt cannot reach the range, no partial band is shown. Cant is assumed, not measured from the game.

    The modern scope rule assumes factory base zero is enabled (FixedBaseZero > 0). Choose “No base zero adjustment” for scopes that disable it; their nominal distance setting does not change the base angle.

    Source-derived, not yet validated against live VR shots. Frame timing, muzzle orientation and scene settings can affect agreement. The model's supported flight behavior and limits are listed above for the selected round.

    To regenerate local data: npm run extract. To serve the app: npm run dev (Node.js 22 or newer). The server listens on all network interfaces; open a printed Network URL from another device on the same LAN. Anyone who can reach that port can read the calculator and its numeric dataset. Extraction requires uv. Raw game files are never served. No external API or account is used.
